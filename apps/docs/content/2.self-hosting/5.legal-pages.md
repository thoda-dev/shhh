Running an instance makes you the data controller for it. The authors of shhh are not: they do not host your instance, cannot read it, and have no access to it. Whatever the law of your country asks of a public website — a privacy policy, a legal notice, terms — it asks of you.

The software gives you somewhere to put those documents. It does not write them for you, and the templates it ships are a starting point, not legal advice.

## What the instance publishes

`/admin/legal` is a super-admin screen holding three documents, each in English and in French:

| Document | Path | Template shipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Privacy policy | `/legal/privacy` | Yes |
| Terms of use | `/legal/terms` | Yes |
| Legal notice | `/legal/notice` | Yes |

**Nothing is published until you write it.** A document that does not exist has no page and no footer link, so an instance you never touch looks exactly as it did before this existed. Publish one and a link appears in the footer for every visitor.

Documents are written in Markdown and stored in the database, next to every other setting — no volume to mount, no file to remember when you back up, and the same admin screen wherever you deploy. Read [Configuration](/self-hosting/configuration) for the reasoning behind that split.

### Languages

Each document is stored per language. A reader gets the document in the language they are browsing in; if you only wrote one language, they get that one, with a notice saying so. Publishing the English version alone is a reasonable place to start — a document in the wrong language beats no document.

## Starting from the template

**Insert the template** opens a form built from the template itself: one field per `{{PLACEHOLDER}}`, most of them showing an example of what belongs there. Nothing in it is validated — an address, a phone number or a contact line is free text, and no format is imposed on you.

Three things the form does:

- **Fills every language at once.** Tick the languages you want and fill the form once. Names, addresses and phone numbers go in verbatim; a duration is a number and a unit, so "30 days" comes out as *30 jours* in the French version; and the few fields that are a sentence rather than a fact — the jurisdiction, the supervisory authority, how you announce changes — get one field per language. Each language lands in its own editor, and one **Save** stores all of them — the line above the button names any language still holding unsaved text.
- **Keeps what you leave empty.** A blank field keeps its `{{PLACEHOLDER}}` in the text, so a half-finished document still says what is missing.
- **Removes what you turn off.** The switch beside a field leaves that information out entirely, and takes its line with it — turn off the postal address and the address line disappears rather than becoming an empty one. A line keeps going only if something else on it survives, so `{{CONTACT_EMAIL}} · {{PHONE}}` without a phone number becomes just the address.

The template fetches nothing from the internet: it lives inside the image you are running, so it works on an instance with no outbound access and always matches your version rather than whatever is on `master`.

The privacy template describes what the software actually processes — IP addresses in sessions and rate limits, account data, recipient addresses on shared pastes, the Cloudflare Turnstile challenge, the audit log — and asks you only for what is specific to you: who runs the instance, who hosts it, who delivers its mail, how long you keep things, and which authority people may complain to.

The terms template covers what follows from the design rather than generic boilerplate: that the link *is* the secret and a lost one is gone for good, that a paste is not storage, that you cannot inspect encrypted content and therefore moderate on report, and that a free instance carries no availability promise. It also holds the usual account, age, suspension, liability and jurisdiction clauses.

::warning
Search the saved text for `{{` before you consider a document published. A policy with placeholders left in it is worse than none: it reads as boilerplate nobody checked.
::

Three of the retention fields arrive already filled from your own configuration: the longest a paste can live comes from the caps in `/admin/settings`, the automatic ban duration from `AUTO_BAN_DURATION_HOURS`, and the session lifetime from the sign-in session itself. Leave a tier uncapped and the paste field starts empty instead, since there is then no maximum to state. The audit log is the one duration nobody can answer for you — nothing purges it, so it is kept until you delete it yourself.

## What Markdown is allowed

Headings, paragraphs, bold and italic, lists, links, quotes, code and tables. That is the whole list; images are not on it, since nothing lets an operator upload one and the CSP loads images from the instance's own origin only. A single newline is kept as a line break, the way it works in a GitHub comment, so an address block stays on several lines without any markup.

Raw HTML and component syntax are **switched off, deliberately**. In an app where the browser holds the only decryption key, a script tag on any page is not a defaced page, it is a leaked key — so the parser never produces one, and link URLs are restricted to `http`, `https` and `mailto`. If you paste HTML into the editor it appears on the page as the characters you typed.

The editor shows the Markdown on the left and the rendered document on the right, updating as you type. That preview goes through the same parser the public page uses, so what you see is what visitors get — including anything the parser drops.

## Do I need a cookie banner?

Out of the box, no.

This instance sets no advertising or analytics cookie. What it does set — the session cookie, the language and colour-mode preferences, and Cloudflare Turnstile's challenge — is either required to deliver what the visitor asked for or required to keep the instance from being abused, which is the category European rules exempt from consent. Exempt from *consent*, not from *disclosure*: the shipped privacy template lists all four in a table, which is what you owe your visitors.

That answer changes the day you put audience measurement in front of the instance, or run it behind something that sets a tracking cookie of its own. Then you need consent, collected before the cookie is set — and the software gives you no banner to do it with.

## Beyond the pages

Two obligations the pages alone do not cover:

- **People who never visited you.** Sharing a paste by email sends a message to an address that its owner never gave you. They are entitled to know where it came from and how long you keep it — the template's *If you are sent a link by email* section says so, but only if you publish it.
- **Deletion.** Account deletion already cascades to pastes, sessions and two-factor secrets, and any account holder can do it themselves from `/account`, with no administrator in the loop. Access and portability requests you answer by hand.

::note
Nothing here is legal advice. If your instance handles anything sensitive, or serves people in a jurisdiction you do not know well, have somebody qualified read what you publish.
::
